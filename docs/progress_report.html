Grid-connected hybrid PV–wind system: islanding test bed and H-infinity current control

Progress report covering Phases 1 to 3 of the workflow  ·  7 October 2026  ·  MATLAB/Simulink R2024b
Summary. The original final-year model was repaired into a working 20 kVA test bed that reaches steady state, tracks maximum power on both sources, and disconnects on islanding. A PI current-control baseline was analysed and simulated from a strong grid down to a short-circuit ratio of 2. A structured H-infinity current controller was then designed and built into the same model. It gives about 3.5 dB more gain margin than PI and a lower sensitivity peak, but no clear improvement in simulated behaviour.

1. Aim and plan

The target is a journal paper on islanding detection for a grid-connected hybrid solar PV–wind system. IEEE 1547-2018 asks an inverter to ride through voltage and frequency disturbances yet still detect a real island within 2 seconds, and passive detectors struggle to tell the two apart.

3. Phase 2: plant model and PI baseline

Plant model

The current controller measures the inverter-side inductor current i1, and the model adds the measured PCC voltage to the controller output (voltage feedforward). With that path closed, the per-phase plant from controller output u to i1 is

G(s) = 1 / ( Z1 + Zc·Z2 / (Zc + Z2 + Zg) )

with Z1 = sL1 + R1, Zc = Rd + 1/(sCf), Z2 = sL2 + R2 and the grid impedance Zg = sLg + Rg set by the short-circuit ratio. This is scripts/plant_model.m.

Conclusion of Phase 2. The PI baseline is stable in steady state down to a short-circuit ratio of 2. It degrades in three ways as the grid weakens: it cannot start at full power below a ratio of about 5, the PCC voltage approaches the 1.10 pu trip limit, and frequency ripple grows from 0.007 to 0.125 Hz.

4. Phase 3: H-infinity current controller

Why a robust design, and which method

The grid inductance is the uncertain quantity: between a short-circuit ratio of 20 and 2 it changes from 1.2 mH to 12.1 mH and moves the plant resonance. Three synthesis routes were tried.

MethodOutcome
Mixed-sensitivity (mixsyn) at one design pointGood at the design point (sensitivity peak 1.31) but unstable at other grid strengths, because it cancels the plant resonance
µ-synthesis (musyn) with uncertain inductanceVery high-order scalings, did not finish in ten minutes, robust performance still above 2 after four iterations
Structured H-infinity (hinfstruct) on five plants at onceAdopted. One fixed-order controller tuned against SCR 20, 10, 5, 3 and 2 simultaneously; no order reduction needed

Design

The synthesis minimises the worst case, over the five plants, of the stacked weighted closed-loop functions:

minK maxplants ‖ [ W1S ;  W2KS ;  W3T ] ‖∞
WeightPurposeSetting
W1 on sensitivity STracking and disturbance rejectionPeak ≤ 1.5, bandwidth 220 Hz, near-integral action at low frequency
W2 on control effort KSLimit high-frequency controller gain1/40 at low frequency, rising above 800 Hz
W3 on complementary sensitivity TRoll-off and robustnessPeak ≤ 1.3, roll-off above 1.5 kHz

Each plant includes a 1.5-sample delay (computation plus PWM). The controller structure is a PI stage in series with a second-order section and a low-pass, order 4 in total, with every parameter bounded so the filter cannot cancel the integrator. An unconstrained third-order filter did exactly that in an earlier attempt.

Tuned valueResultTuned valueResult
Proportional gain4.00Section numerator damping0.61

The worst-case weighted norm is 1.27, so the targets are almost but not fully met. The controller is discretised with the Tustin method at 10 kHz and stored in scripts/hinf_controller.mat.

Implementation in Simulink

The current control of both axes is now sampled at the switching frequency, in step with the PWM carrier, and its output is applied one sample later. Each axis has one block holding the PI controller, the H-infinity controller, a selector and the computation delay. The parameter P.ctrl.useHinf selects PI (0) or H-infinity (1), so both controllers are tested in the same model.

Reading of the Phase 3 result.
  • The H-infinity controller is more robust on paper: about 3.5 dB more gain margin and a sensitivity peak of 1.4 to 1.5 against 1.8 to 1.9.
  • In simulation the behaviour is nearly the same. Distortion is slightly lower with H-infinity at SCR 3 and 2 and higher at SCR 10 and 5; the DC-link response is identical.
  • The synthesis converged close to a lower-gain PI (gain 4 against 6, with an almost flat filter). A PI retuned to that gain would perform about the same.
  • Neither controller survives a start at full power at SCR 3. That points to the PLL, not the current loop.
PI therefore remains the default controller in the model. Whether the H-infinity design belongs in the paper as a contribution is an open decision.

6. Limitations

  • All results are simulation only; there is no hardware or hardware-in-the-loop validation.
  • PI and PLL gains are a first pass and were not optimised, so the comparison is against a reasonable PI, not the best possible one.
  • The plant model treats each axis as a single loop and does not include the PLL, which is where the weak-grid start-up failure appears to come from.
  • Generator stator inductance (3 mH) and rotor inertia (10 kg·m²) were changed from the original so the machine can deliver 10 kW through a diode rectifier.
  • PV current readings are not reliable when the PV voltage is far above its operating point, including after a trip. This has not been explained.
  • About 10% of generated power does not reach the grid; the losses have not been broken down.
  • The passive detector's settings are not tuned and it has no time delays.

7. Next steps

  1. Decide whether the H-infinity controller stays in the paper, is reduced to a short note, or is dropped.
  2. Phase 4: map the non-detection zone of the passive detector over active and reactive power mismatch, give it realistic time delays, and add one active detection method as a second baseline.
  3. Phase 5: generate the labelled dataset of islanding and non-islanding events, with scopes switched off for speed.
  4. Phases 6 and 7: train the classifier and validate it in closed loop against both baselines.
